## 3. "Suspiciously clean" analysis

This bundle is the opposite of suspiciously clean — it is adversarial *against* the product being graded: it preserves a 55-poll failure log verbatim, files five S1/S2 defects against the flagship feature, self-reports the tester's own error (Windows codepage misread initially suspected as a data bug, then retracted with file-level verification), and repeatedly separates narrated claims from raw artifacts. Every PASS I re-derived held against its trace, and the one internal contradiction I found (D-20's citation) cuts against evidence hygiene, not against the product. What is missing that I would expect: any UI-level evidence (the entire journey is API traces — a writer never *saw* the sidebar or a flag), the intermediate alias dump, an empirical cross-USER probe for D-30 (asserted structurally only), the timeline endpoint's user-facing symptom, any export exercise, and the auto-clear-on-fix lifecycle (code-verified only, by admission). All but the UI gap are self-disclosed by the executors, which raises credibility. Residual bias risk: severity *framings* ("moat-critical", "S1 candidate") are the graded party's rhetoric — I re-derived severities from the traces and they mostly hold.

## 4. Overall impression

The continuity moat is real — this bundle proves it catching two error classes no competitor attempts, on live LLM extraction — but it is wrapped in silently-failing plumbing that loses derived data, writes into sibling books, hides its own outages behind clean 200s, and rolls character state backward, so the persona's core promise is today more demo than guarantee.
