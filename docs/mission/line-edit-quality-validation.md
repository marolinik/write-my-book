**Book:** *The Salt Letters* (`4a37715f-30ad-43d9-9960-3ba9c0d169a4`)
**Date:** 2026-07-05
**Method:** Read the real FINGERPRINT document; pulled every existing `line-editor` finding
(ch5); ran ONE fresh live line-edit on ch3 (`session 97e92b21`, sonnet) to enlarge the

**The line-editor fights the fingerprint.** It understands the voice — it quotes the
fingerprint in its own rationales — and then flags the voice as a flaw anyway. Six of eight
suggestions would damage the prose if applied blindly, and each of the six targets a device
the fingerprint *explicitly names as a deliberate signature*: the "two-things-at-once"
paradox, the `and`-stacked accretive tide-rhythm, the ritual/log inventory, and the dry
inference-from-physical-detail wit. The single clean win is a trivial preposition-level echo.

The machinery to do better already exists (the fingerprint, voice-metrics, and calibration
samples are all injected into the line-editor's context, and the **dev-editor** on the same
book actively cites the fingerprint to *protect* maritime metaphors). The line-editor fails
anyway because its 23 generic craft checks **out-rank** the diffuse "preserve voice"
instruction. This is a precedence bug, not a context bug — and it is a cheap, targeted fix.

## The voice it is supposed to protect (from the real FINGERPRINT doc)

Quoting the fingerprint's own "Recurring Stylistic Signatures" and rules:

- **Signature move — the contradictory truth:** *"I understood two things at once: my
  brother was dead, and my brother was still writing to me." / "Two things now true at
  once."* — "This becomes a **structural and thematic refrain**."
- **Accretion, not subordination:** long sentences "do so through **accretion** … phrases
  stack like stones," joined with `and`. "The length serves observation … not decoration."
- **Inventory as ritual:** *"Coffee at five. The log at six — wind speed, visibility …"* —
  "Precision as survival mechanism."
- **Emotion enacted, never named**; the narrator draws inference from physical detail
  (*"his eyes went … quick, like a tongue to a sore tooth"*) — "trust the reader to infer."
- **Dry, understated wit** with "a **bite**"; deliberate ambiguity that must **not** be
  resolved; em-dashes; NO purple prose, NO exclamation, NO "suddenly."

These are exactly the constructions the line-editor keeps flagging.

## Before / After evidence

Labels: **FLATTEN** = homogenizes prose, kills a signature, or resolves deliberate
ambiguity. **SHARPEN** = fixes a genuine weakness while respecting voice.

### 1. FLATTEN — the wick paradox (ch5) — *the single most damning example*

> **Original:** "the way a lamp burns when the wick is overfed **and hungry and** not
> trimmed back to its **right and** narrow place."
>
> **Line-editor's rewrite:** "the way a lamp burns when the wick is overfed and not trimmed
> back to its narrow place."
>
> **Line-editor's reason:** "the cascading `and…and` construction … **weaken[s] the punch**."

This one suggestion attacks *both* of the fingerprint's two most-named signatures at once:
it deletes **"hungry"** (the *overfed **and** hungry* = "two things at once" paradox) and it
strips the **`and`-stacked accretion** the fingerprint calls the deliberate tide-rhythm. It
also kills "**right and** narrow" — a witty play on *straight-and-narrow*.

**The author independently rejected it**, and her dismiss-reason is the verdict in one line:

> *"The wick paradox is load-bearing: overfed AND hungry is the whole point, and 'right and
> narrow' is a deliberate echo of the strait-and-narrow. Trimming … **flattens** the
> biblical resonance and **kills the paradox**. Voice over smoothness here."*

This is the same failure the team already saw live (the line-editor calling the "accumulation
of coordinating conjunctions" draggy). It is systemic, not a one-off.

### 2. FLATTEN — deletes a whole signature sentence (ch3, fresh run)

> **Original:** `"We imagine he was carrying payment," said the soft-handed one, and his
> voice was softer than his hands, which is how I understood he was not from any bank that
> keeps its name on a door.`
>
> **Line-editor's rewrite:** `"We imagine he was carrying payment," said the soft-handed one.`

It deletes ~20 words of pure voice down to a bare dialogue tag. "his voice was softer than
his hands" is the fingerprint's dry-wit-plus-physical-detail move; "not from any bank that
keeps its name on a door" is the exact understated menace the fingerprint prizes.
Tellingly, the editor's **own rationale admits the line works** — *"the comparison already
signals his non-corporate nature"* — then cuts it anyway, filed under "show vs. tell."

### 3. FLATTEN + category error — the log ritual read as a "filter word" (ch3, fresh run)

> **Original:** `That night I wrote in the log: "NNE 9 knots. Vis good. Two men, mainland.
> Asked the wrong question of the right woman." Then I drew the line under it…`
>
> **Line-editor's rewrite:** `"NNE 9 knots. Vis good. Two men, mainland…" I drew the line
> under it…` (deletes "That night I wrote in the log")

Writing in the log is a physical **action** and a **ritual**, not a perception filter-word
(filter words are *saw / heard / felt*). The cut also destroys the "**That night**" time-
marker the fingerprint explicitly celebrates. Most damning: the editor's **own rationale
quotes the fingerprint's ritual passage** — *"The log is a character in its own right … puts
the reader inside Vera's ritual"* — while proposing to remove the ritual's frame. It sees
the signature, names it, and guts it. **Proof that injecting the fingerprint is not enough.**

### 4. FLATTEN — literalizes a witty paradox and breaks the callback (ch3, fresh run)

> **Original:** "The question had asked itself, which is the village's other export."
>
> **Line-editor's rewrite:** "The answer presented itself, which is the village's other
> export." ("'The question had asked itself' is a confusing metaphor.")

"The question asked itself" is a deliberate dry paradox that callbacks the chapter's opening
conceit (*"Intelligence is the village's only reliable export"*). Changing it to "the answer
presented itself" reads the figure of speech **literally**, flattens the wit, and breaks the
"export" joke (the village exports the *question*, not the answer). The editor's rationale
even reconstructs the joke correctly — then overrides it.

### 5. FLATTEN — cuts characterizing accretion from dialogue (ch3, fresh run)

> **Original:** "Your brother paid it in January, which surprised the whole co-op, because
> he paid in April most years, **with a story about the winter catch**."
>
> **Line-editor's rewrite:** "Your brother paid it in January — after years of April
> payments, which surprised the co-op."

Tighter, yes — but it deletes "with a story about the winter catch," a concrete detail that
characterizes Danilo (a man who always had an excuse) and rides the accretive dialogue
rhythm. Trades character for smoothness.

### 6. FLATTEN — resolves deliberate self-correcting recursion (ch5)

> **Original:** "…even though I was nine and you were lecturing and I was not listening
> **except I was**."
>
> **Line-editor:** flagged as a "clarity" stumble (and proposed **no actual change** — the
> `newText` equals the original).

Author's dismiss-reason: *"The recursion … is deliberate characterization of Danilo
correcting himself mid-thought; the stumble IS the voice."* The editor flagged a signature as
a defect and produced a no-op "fix."

### 7. BORDERLINE — regional clip sanded (ch3, fresh run)

> **Original:** "The co-op put the deposit through Tuesday."  → **"…through on Tuesday."**

A defensible micro-clarity call, but it standardizes Radmila's clipped regional cadence
(the fingerprint: "regional rhythm without … heavy dialect"). Minor.

### 8. SHARPEN — the one clean win (ch5)

> **Original:** "It was wedged **under** the same stone I had found the second letter
> **under**, except this time…"  → **"…the same stone that had held the second letter…"**

A genuine accidental prepositional stutter (double "under"), not a deliberate refrain.
Fixing it respects the voice. **The author applied this one.** This is the line-editor at its
best — and it is the *only* finding of the eight that clears the bar.

## Why it flattens (root-cause diagnosis)

Read the line-editor system prompt in
**`src/lib/agents/prompt-assembler.ts`** (the `"line-editor"` role block, ~lines 359-469).

1. **The 23 checks are generic and several collide head-on with this book's signatures.**
   - `#1 Sentence variety — Monotonous patterns?` → collides with the deliberate tide-rhythm
     of repeated short declaratives.
   - `#2 Crutch phrases — Repeated filler?` and `#6 Echoes — same word repeated in close
     proximity?` → collide with the `and`-stacked accretion **and** the intentional refrains
     ("two things at once", "export").
   - `#9 Show vs. tell` and `#20 Over-explanation` → collide with testimony narration and the
     narrator's signature inference-from-physical-detail.
   - `#3 Filter words` → misfires on the log ritual (an action, not a perception verb).

2. **There is no "protected signature devices" carve-out.** The prompt does say "preserve
   this voice" and "NEVER homogenize toward … generic," but that is a *diffuse exhortation*.
   The 23 checks are *concrete, numbered, actionable imperatives*. When the two conflict — and
   for this voice they conflict precisely on the signatures — **the concrete checks win.**

3. **It is not a context gap.** The fingerprint is injected `full`
   (`prompt-assembler.ts:1602`), and `voice_metrics` + `calibration_samples` are injected for
   editing agents (`~:1617-1662`). The findings above *quote the fingerprint in their own
   rationales* and still fire. The craft skills the line-editor receives
   (`skills/index.ts:27` → `AI_TELL_DETECTION`; `advanced-craft.ts:425` →
   `DIALOGUE_MASTERY` + `SELF_EDITING_CHECKLIST`) only *reinforce* generic tightening
   ("no excessive … passive voice, filter words"; "was/were frequency"; "adverb count
   decreasing is good") — none of them protect intentional accretion or refrain.

So: the signatures are present in context but have **no precedence** over the checklist.

## Recommended fix (specific, minimal, shippable)

**File:** `src/lib/agents/prompt-assembler.ts` — the `"line-editor"` role prompt (lines
359-469).

**Change 1 (primary, ~15 lines).** Insert a hard gate *before* the 23 checks (right after
the `## STYLE-AWARE EDITING` block, ~line 375):

```
## PROTECTED SIGNATURE DEVICES — READ BEFORE JUDGING (this OUTRANKS the 23 checks)
The <style_fingerprint> names the author's recurring, DELIBERATE devices (see its
"Recurring Stylistic Signatures" / "AI Mitigation" sections). A deliberate device is
NEVER a finding. Standing exemptions for THIS voice:
- Coordinating-conjunction / "and"-stacking (accretion) is the author's tide-rhythm.
  It is NOT a crutch phrase (#2), an echo (#6), or a run-on.
- A word or phrase repeated for effect (a refrain) is NOT an echo (#6).
- A compressed paradox ("two things at once") or self-correcting recursion is NOT
  over-explanation (#20) or a clarity defect — even if the reader pauses.
- Ritual/log/inventory lines and precise time-markers ("That night") are voice, not
  filter-words (#3) or telling (#9). Recording an action ("I wrote in the log") is an
  ACTION, not a perception filter-word.
- The narrator inferring meaning from a physical detail ("which is how I understood…")
  is the CORE move of this voice, not over-explanation.

GATE: before every CreateFinding, ask — does the anchorQuote instantiate a device the
fingerprint names or exemplifies as deliberate? If yes, DO NOT create the finding unless
there is a true execution error *inside* the device (e.g. an accidental double preposition),
and state which fingerprint rule permits the edit.
```

Also add a 6-word tag to checks #2 and #6, e.g. `#6 Echoes — same word repeated in close
proximity? (exempt intentional refrains and the author's accretive rhythm — see PROTECTED
SIGNATURE DEVICES)`.

**Why this works:** it converts "preserve voice" from a diffuse wish into a *numbered
imperative that explicitly out-ranks the checks*, and it names the exact collisions the model
is currently getting wrong. Every one of the six FLATTEN findings above is caught by this
gate. The one true SHARPEN (double "under") still passes, because it is an execution error
*inside* — not an instance of — a signature.

**Change 2 (robust follow-up).** Have the **style-analyst** emit a structured
`signatureDevices: [{ name, verbatimExample, doNotFlag: true }]` array on the `StyleProfile`
(it already writes calibration samples), and inject it beside `<style_fingerprint>` as a
`<protected_signatures>` block. This makes the protection machine-explicit and per-book,
instead of relying on the model to re-derive it from prose each run. Change 1 is enough to
stop the bleeding today; Change 2 makes it durable.

---

## Bottom line

The line-editor is a competent generic prose checker pointed at a voice whose signatures
*are* the things generic checkers flag. It preserves voice only by accident (1/8), and it
actively recommends deleting the book's best, most characteristic lines — the paradoxes, the
accretion, the ritual, the dry wit — often while quoting the fingerprint that forbids exactly
that. Grade **D+**. The fix is a ~15-line precedence gate in one prompt, plus an optional
structured-signatures follow-up. Until then, the line-editor's suggestions should be treated
as "smoothness" proposals to be *resisted* on this book, not applied.

## CORRECTION (same session) — the D+ was CONFOUNDED; the gate actually works

The original D+ grade and the "prose gate is ineffective" re-validation were both **invalidated by an
environment bug**: FIVE stale `worker.ts` processes were running concurrently (started at different
times today), several on **pre-gate code** (`tsx` has no hot-reload), all draining the same Redis queue.
Line-edit jobs were being grabbed by a *random* worker — often an old-code one **without** the gate. So
the re-validations never actually tested the fix.

After killing all 5 stale workers and running **exactly one** fresh worker on the gate code (model
cost-confirmed as qwen3.6, not Claude):

| | Confounded baseline | Clean single-worker |
|---|---|---|
| **Grade** | D+ | **B-** |
| **Signature devices flattened** | ~6 of 6 | **2 of 6** |
| **Devices correctly protected** | ~1 | **4 of 6** (paradox, log-ritual, refrains, recursion; ch5 = 0 flattens) |

The gate **works**: qwen now emits its own "Signature-Protected Devices (NOT flagged)" section quoting
the fingerprint, converting wholesale flattening into surgical, fingerprint-aware editing. Residual leak
= the **2 most central devices** (the accretive "and"-stack and the inference-anchor), where qwen
rationalizes its *own* carve-out ("three items can dilute") and flags anyway. Closing those 2 → A- is
the job of **Change 2** (structured, absolute `signatureDevices` that outrank even the model's own
reasoning), or routing voice-critical edits to a stronger model.

**Operational finding:** the 5-stale-worker accumulation was caused by `TaskStop` not killing the OS
process (dev-only; prod uses supervised single processes). Also observed, as minor follow-ups: a
leftover "Test finding" junk row in `edit_findings`, a findings **dedup bug** (6 near-identical rows for
one finding), and one finding whose rationale misquotes its own anchor.
