The boathouse ledger was where my brother kept his other arithmetic.

I found it on the second shelf, behind the tins of paint that had not been opened since my father's time, wrapped in oilcloth and tied with the kind of knot a man uses when he means to come back. The pages smelled of paraffin. The entries began four years ago, in March, and they were not the co-op's entries: no catch weights, no fuel, no ice. Other arithmetic. His.

Names I did not know, and sums I did, because the sums repeated the way a tide repeats — two hundred in, two hundred out, and then in October a column that broke the pattern and kept breaking it, five hundred and eight hundred and eleven hundred and four thousand.

Four thousand. The number had followed me down the harbor road and up the ninety-six steps and now it sat in my brother's cramped hand on a page that had never been meant for me, or had been meant for me the whole time, which with Danilo was not a distinction but a method.

I read the ledger twice at the boathouse table with the door open to the water. Then I wrote in my own log, because the habit holds when nothing else does: "Ledger found. Forty-one entries. One name I know." I drew no line under it. The ledger was not finished with me, and I do not close what is still speaking.

The name I knew was Kova's.

He was mending pots on the mole that afternoon, and he did not stop mending them when my shadow crossed the twine, which is how I knew he had been waiting for me for some days. A man who is surprised looks up. A man who has rehearsed does not.

"You found the book," he said.

"I found a book. Whether it is the book depends on what you were hoping was in it."

He tied off a mesh and cut the twine with his thumbnail, and the pause was the length of a man deciding which truth was cheapest. "Your brother moved things. Small things, early. Then the things got heavier and the money got quieter. I told him the sea does not keep secrets, it only keeps salvage."

"He wrote your name eleven times."

"Then he was honest eleven times," Kova said, "which for Danilo was a record, and you know it."

I did know it. I had come down the steps to be angry at somebody, and the somebody kept turning out to be my brother, and the anger kept arriving with its edges already worn, the way sea glass arrives: dangerous once, and then handled by so much water that all you can do is hold it up to the light.

That night the wind backed westerly and the pressure held at 1015 and I wrote: "W 6 knots. Vis good. One name, eleven entries. He was honest eleven times." Then I drew the line.
